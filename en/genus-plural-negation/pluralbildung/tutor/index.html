Context: the page “Noun Plurals”

Form common German noun plurals and use them with the plural article die.

Peter Unpacks His Boxes

Peter's moving boxes hold more than one book and one photo! In the plural, every German noun uses the definite article die — whether it was der, die, or das in the singular. There is no indefinite article in the plural.

The Five Most Common Plural Patterns:

  • Ending | Singular → Plural | Typical for
  • -n / -en | die Lampe → die Lampen | most feminine nouns
  • -e (often + umlaut) | der Stuhl → die Stühle | many masc./neuter nouns
  • -er (often + umlaut) | das Buch → die Bücher | short neuter nouns
  • -s | das Foto → die Fotos | words ending in a, i, o, u & loanwords
  • — (often + umlaut) | das Zimmer → die Zimmer | nouns ending in -er, -el, -en, -chen

Vocabulary (German – English): Key Plurals for Peter's Room

  • der Tisch, die Tische – table
  • der Stuhl, die Stühle – chair
  • die Lampe, die Lampen – lamp
  • das Buch, die Bücher – book
  • das Foto, die Fotos – photo
  • das Zimmer, die Zimmer – room

Exercise plural-muster-zuordnen

  • Task: Match each singular noun with its correct plural form.
  • Answer format: match each item on the left with one on the right
  • Solution: die Tasche – die Taschen (-n); der Ball – die Bälle (umlaut + -e); das Kind – die Kinder (-er); das Handy – die Handys (-s); das Zimmer – die Zimmer (—)
  • Explanation: The five most common plural patterns are -n/-en, -e, -er, -s, and no ending (often with an umlaut).

Exercise plural-schreiben

  • Task: Type the matching plural form with the article die in each gap.
  • Answer format: fill in the gaps in German
  • How the app counts mistakes of form: capital letters right with a hint, accents wrong, hyphen wrong, apostrophe wrong, punctuation ignored
  • Items (start → solution):
  1. The books are on the shelf. → Die Bücher stehen im Regal.
    • Die Buchs: das Buch forms its plural with an umlaut and -er: die Bücher.
  2. The chairs are new. → Die Stühle sind neu.
    • Die Stuhle: der Stuhl takes an umlaut in the plural: die Stühle.
  3. The lamps are bright. → Die Lampen sind hell.
  4. The photos are from home. → Die Fotos sind von zu Hause.
  • Explanation: Plural always uses die. Watch for umlauts (Stühle, Bücher) and common endings (-n, -e, -er, -s).
  • How to help: the app checks word by word against the solutions above and names mistakes of form (e.g. accents) with the child's words. Do not give the solution: ask about the rule (ending, gender, tense) or about a similar sentence of the page. If the app takes a version of the child's as wrong that you think is right, tell the child: the app knows only the versions above.

Exercise plural-artikel-regel

  • Task: Which statement about German plurals is true?
  • Answer format: choose one answer
  • Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
    • ✓ In the nominative plural, all nouns take the definite article die. – Correct! All three singular genders merge under die in the plural.
    • ✗ Masculine nouns keep the article der in the plural. – No, masculine nouns like der Stuhl also change to die Stühle in the plural.
    • ✗ Every German noun forms its plural by adding -s. – Only certain words (like Fotos, Autos) take -s. Most plurals use -n/-en, -e, -er, or no ending.
  • Solution: In the nominative plural, all nouns take the definite article die.
  • Explanation: Nominative plural always takes die, and the plural ending depends on the noun.

Exercise plural-ohne-endung-check

  • Task: Which of these nouns take no new ending in the plural (— pattern)?
  • Answer format: choose all right answers
  • Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
    • ✓ das Brötchen → die Brötchen – Correct! Nouns ending in -chen stay unchanged in the plural.
    • ✓ der Lehrer → die Lehrer – Correct! Many nouns ending in -er take no extra ending in the plural.
    • ✗ die Schule → die Schulen – die Schule adds -n: die Schulen.
    • ✗ das Buch → die Bücher – das Buch adds an umlaut and -er: die Bücher.
  • Solution: das Brötchen → die Brötchen; der Lehrer → die Lehrer
  • Explanation: Nouns ending in -er, -el, -en, -chen often take no plural ending (die Lehrer, die Brötchen, die Zimmer).
