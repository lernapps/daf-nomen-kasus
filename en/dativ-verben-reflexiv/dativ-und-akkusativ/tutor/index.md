## Context: the page “Dative and Accusative Objects”

Build sentences with both a dative recipient and an accusative object in the right order.

### Recipient + Thing: The Arrow Diagram

Verbs like **geben, schenken, zeigen, bringen, leihen, schreiben** often take two objects: the **recipient** in the dative (to whom?) and the **thing** in the accusative (what?). Their word order depends on whether they are nouns or pronouns.

Picture: Arrow diagram: Peter (Nominative) schenkt -> der Freundin / ihr (Dative, recipient) and ein Spiel / es (Accusative, thing). Rules: 2 nouns Dative before Accusative; 1 pronoun goes first; 2 pronouns Accusative before Dative. Caption: Three clear word-order rules for sentences with both dative and accusative objects.

The 3 Word-Order Rules:
- Combination | Order | Example
- 2 nouns | Dative → Accusative | Peter schenkt der Freundin ein Spiel.
- 1 pronoun + 1 noun | Pronoun first | Peter schenkt ihr ein Spiel. / Peter schenkt es Nadia.
- 2 pronouns | Accusative → Dative | Peter schenkt es ihr.

### Exercise zwei-objekte-ordnen

- Task: Put the sentence parts in the right order: *Peter lends his friend the bike.*
- Answer format: put the items in the right order (the app shows them shuffled)
- Solution: 1. Peter, 2. leiht, 3. dem Freund, 4. das Fahrrad.
- Explanation: With two nouns, the dative (*dem Freund*) comes before the accusative (*das Fahrrad*).

### Exercise zwei-pronomen-wahl

- Task: Choose the right object order with pronouns.
- Answer format: choose a word for each gap
- How the app counts mistakes of form: capital letters wrong, accents wrong, hyphen wrong, apostrophe wrong, punctuation wrong
- Items (start → solution):
1. Peter gives it (das Buch) to her (Mina). → Peter gibt es ihr.
2. Lukas shows it (den Ball) to Peter. → Lukas zeigt ihn Peter.
3. Nadia writes him (Lukas) a card. → Nadia schreibt ihm eine Karte.
- Explanation: A single pronoun comes before a noun (*ihn Peter*, *ihm eine Karte*). With two pronouns, accusative comes before dative (*es ihr*).
- How to help: the app checks word by word against the solutions above and names mistakes of form (e.g. accents) with the child's words. ### Exercise zwei-objekte-luecken

- Task: Type both objects in the right order.
- Answer format: fill in the gaps in German
- How the app counts mistakes of form: capital letters right with a hint, accents wrong, hyphen wrong, apostrophe wrong, punctuation ignored
- Items (start → solution):
1. Peter gives the teacher (der Lehrer) the notebook (das Heft). → Peter gibt dem Lehrer das Heft.
     - das Heft dem Lehrer: With two nouns, dative (*dem Lehrer*) comes before accusative (*das Heft*).
2. Lukas brings it (das Buch) to him (Peter). → Lukas bringt es ihm.
     - ihm es: With two pronouns, accusative (*es*) comes before dative (*ihm*).
3. Mina gives her (Nadia) the ball (der Ball). → Mina gibt ihr den Ball.
- Explanation: Remember: 2 nouns = Dat → Acc; 1 pronoun = pronoun first; 2 pronouns = Acc → Dat.
- How to help: the app checks word by word against the solutions above and names mistakes of form (e.g. accents) with the child's words. Do not give the solution: ask about the rule (ending, gender, tense) or about a similar sentence of the page. ### Exercise zwei-pronomen-regel-check

- Task: When both objects are pronouns (such as es and ihr), the dative pronoun always comes first.
- Answer format: choose “true” or “false”
- Solution: The statement is false.
- Explanation: False! With two pronouns, **accusative comes before dative**: *Peter gibt **es ihr**.*
