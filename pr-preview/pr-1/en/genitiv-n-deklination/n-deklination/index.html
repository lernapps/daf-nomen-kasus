The Pattern Card for Weak Masculine Nouns

At a neighborhood party in Lindenberg, Peter meets a colleague of Herr Weber, the neighbor, and a university student. A special group of masculine nouns — the n-declension — adds -n or -en in every case except nominative singular!

Key Weak Masculine Nouns
GermanExample · noteEnglish
der Mensch, den/dem/des Menschenperson / human being
der Kollege, den/dem/des Kollegencolleague (male)
der Student, den/dem/des Studentenuniversity student (male)
der Nachbar, den/dem/des Nachbarnneighbor (male)
der Junge, den/dem/des Jungenboy
der Herr, den/dem/des Herrngentleman / Mr.
der Name, den/dem Namen, des Namens

In the genitive, an extra -s is added: des Namens.

Practice

Exercise

Fill in the right form of each weak masculine noun.

  1. Peter meets a colleague (der Kollege, acc.) of Herr Weber. → Peter trifft einen … von Herr Weber.
  2. Lukas helps the neighbor (der Nachbar, dat.) in the garden. → Lukas hilft dem … im Garten.
  3. That is the backpack of the student (der Student, gen.). → Das ist der Rucksack des ….
  4. Do you know the name (der Name, acc.) of the street? → Kennst du den … der Straße?
Solution
  1. Peter trifft einen Kollegen von Herr Weber.
  2. Lukas hilft dem Nachbarn im Garten.
  3. Das ist der Rucksack des Studenten.
  4. Kennst du den Namen der Straße?

Weak masculine nouns like Kollege, Nachbar, Student, Mensch, Junge, Name take -n or -en in accusative, dative, and genitive.

Exercise

Which masculine noun does **not** belong to the n-declension?

  • der Lehrer (den Lehrer, dem Lehrer, des Lehrers) right answer

    Correct! *der Lehrer* is a regular masculine noun (genitive *des Lehrers*, no *-n* in accusative/dative).

  • der Mensch (den Menschen, dem Menschen, des Menschen)

    *der Mensch* is a classic weak masculine noun.

  • der Kollege (den Kollegen, dem Kollegen, des Kollegen)

    Masculine nouns for people ending in *-e* like *der Kollege* or *der Junge* belong to the n-declension.

The n-declension mostly includes masculine people/animals ending in *-e* (*Kollege, Junge*) plus key nouns like *Mensch, Student, Nachbar, Herr*.
