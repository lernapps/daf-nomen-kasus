Can you already do this?

For German cases and negation, you build on two basics: Personal pronouns (nominative) (I can use German nominative personal pronouns — ich, du, er, sie, es, wir, ihr, sie, Sie — as sentence subjects) and Verb-second word order (I can build German statements with the conjugated verb in second position, including inversion after time or place).

Exercise

Which sentence from Peter's daily life in Lindenberg uses correct verb-second word order and the right subject pronoun?

  • Heute wohnt er bei Familie Weber in Lindenberg. right answer

    Correct! After the time expression *Heute*, the conjugated verb *wohnt* stays in position 2, followed by the subject *er*.

  • Heute er wohnt bei Familie Weber in Lindenberg.

    Here the verb is in third position. In a German main-clause statement, the conjugated verb stays in position 2.

  • Heute wohnt ihn bei Familie Weber in Lindenberg.

    *ihn* is not a nominative subject pronoun. The subject needs *er*.

In a German statement, the conjugated verb always takes second position, and the subject uses the nominative case.
