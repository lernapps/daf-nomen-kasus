Sam Unpacks His Boxes

Sam's moving boxes hold more than one book and one photo! In the plural, every German noun uses the definite article die — whether it was der, die, or das in the singular. There is no indefinite article in the plural.

The Five Most Common Plural Patterns
EndingSingular → PluralTypical for
-n / -endie Lampe → die Lampenmost feminine nouns
-e (often + umlaut)der Stuhl → die Stühlemany masc./neuter nouns
-er (often + umlaut)das Buch → die Büchershort neuter nouns
-sdas Foto → die Fotoswords ending in a, i, o, u & loanwords
— (often + umlaut)das Zimmer → die Zimmernouns ending in -er, -el, -en, -chen

Practice

Exercise

Type the matching plural form with the article die in each gap.

  1. The books are on the shelf. → … stehen im Regal.
  2. The chairs are new. → … sind neu.
  3. The lamps are bright. → … sind hell.
  4. The photos are from home. → … sind von zu Hause.
Solution
  1. Die Bücher stehen im Regal.
  2. Die Stühle sind neu.
  3. Die Lampen sind hell.
  4. Die Fotos sind von zu Hause.

Plural always uses die. Watch for umlauts (Stühle, Bücher) and common endings (-n, -e, -er, -s).

Exercise

Which statement about German plurals is true?

  • In the nominative plural, all nouns take the definite article die. right answer

    Correct! All three singular genders merge under *die* in the plural.

  • Masculine nouns keep the article der in the plural.

    No, masculine nouns like *der Stuhl* also change to *die Stühle* in the plural.

  • Every German noun forms its plural by adding -s.

    Only certain words (like *Fotos, Autos*) take *-s*. Most plurals use *-n/-en, -e, -er*, or no ending.

Nominative plural always takes *die*, and the plural ending depends on the noun.
