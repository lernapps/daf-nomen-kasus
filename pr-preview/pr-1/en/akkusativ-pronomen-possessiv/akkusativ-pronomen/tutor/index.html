Context: the page “Accusative Personal Pronouns”

Use accusative personal pronouns (mich, dich, ihn, sie, es, uns, euch, sie, Sie) as direct objects.

Stand-in Cards in the Accusative

Instead of repeating a noun or name, Sam and Nadia use Stand-in Cards (personal pronouns). When the pronoun is the direct object (whom or what?), you use the accusative form: Kennst du Lukas? Ja, ich kenne ihn!

Personal Pronouns: Nominative → Accusative:

  • Nominative | Accusative | Example
  • ich / du | mich / dich | Nadia ruft mich an. Ich sehe dich.
  • er / sie / es | ihn / sie / es | Ich kaufe ihn (den Ball), sie (die Lampe), es (das Buch).
  • wir / ihr | uns / euch | Herr Braun fragt uns. Wir besuchen euch.
  • sie (pl) / Sie | sie / Sie | Ich kenne sie (die Bücher). Ich frage Sie.

Remember: Things Use Pronouns Too!

In German, the pronoun matches the noun's grammatical gender, not whether it is a person or a thing: der Rucksack → ihn, die Gitarre → sie, das Regal → es.

Exercise pronomen-nom-akk-zuordnen

  • Task: Match each nominative card with its accusative stand-in card.
  • Answer format: match each item on the left with one on the right
  • Solution: ich – mich; du – dich; er (der Stuhl) – ihn; wir – uns; ihr – euch
  • Explanation: In the accusative, ich→mich, du→dich, er→ihn, wir→uns, ihr→euch change; sie, es, Sie stay unchanged.

Exercise akkusativ-pronomen-ersetzen

  • Task: Replace the noun or person with the right accusative pronoun.
  • Answer format: fill in the gaps in German
  • How the app counts mistakes of form: capital letters wrong, accents wrong, hyphen wrong, apostrophe wrong, punctuation wrong
  • Items (start → solution):
  1. Where is the backpack (der Rucksack)? I am looking for it. → Wo ist der Rucksack? Ich suche ihn.
    • es: der Rucksack is masculine — in German, replace it with ihn, not es.
  2. Nadia visits us in Lindenberg. → Nadia besucht uns in Lindenberg.
  3. Do you see the lamp (die Lampe)? Yes, I see it. → Siehst du die Lampe? Ja, ich sehe sie.
  4. Sam asks me about the homework. → Sam fragt mich nach den Hausaufgaben.
    • mir: fragen takes the accusative → mich (not dative mir).
  • Explanation: For things, match the grammatical gender (der→ihn, die→sie, das→es); for people, use the accusative form (mich, dich, ihn, sie, uns, euch).
  • How to help: the app checks word by word against the solutions above and names mistakes of form (e.g. accents) with the child's words. Do not give the solution: ask about the rule (ending, gender, tense) or about a similar sentence of the page. If the app takes a version of the child's as wrong that you think is right, tell the child: the app knows only the versions above.
