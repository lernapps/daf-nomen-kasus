## Context: the page “Verbs with Dative”

Use common dative verbs such as helfen, gefallen, gehören, danken, schmecken, and passen.

### Verbs That Directly Require Dative

Some German verbs take **no accusative object** at all — they always require the **dative**, even when English uses a direct object (*help me → hilf **mir***!). Learn these core dative verbs as a group.

Vocabulary (German – English): Core Dative Verbs in Lindenberg
- helfen – to help. Example: Lukas hilft mir bei den Hausaufgaben. – Lukas helps me with homework.
- gefallen – to please / to be liked by. Example: Das Zimmer gefällt ihm sehr. – He really likes the room.
- gehören – to belong to. Example: Das Fahrrad gehört der Schwester. – The bike belongs to the sister.
- danken – to thank. Example: Peter dankt den Eltern. – Peter thanks the parents.
- schmecken – to taste good to. Example: Die Brötchen schmecken uns. – We enjoy the bread rolls.
- passen – to fit / to suit. Example: Die Jacke passt dir gut. – The jacket fits you well.

**Remember: Watch the Subject with gefallen and schmecken!**

With *gefallen* and *schmecken*, **the thing is the subject** and the person is in the dative: *Das Buch (subj., sg.) **gefällt** mir.* — *Die Bücher (subj., pl.) **gefallen** mir.*

### Exercise dativverben-luecken

- Task: Fill in the dative pronoun or article after each dative verb.
- Answer format: fill in the gaps in German
- How the app counts mistakes of form: capital letters wrong, accents wrong, hyphen wrong, apostrophe wrong, punctuation wrong
- Items (start → solution):
1. Nadia helps me (ich) with German. → Nadia hilft mir beim Deutschlernen.
     - mich: *helfen* is a dative verb → always *mir*, never *mich*.
2. The bike belongs to the teacher (der Lehrer). → Das Fahrrad gehört dem Lehrer.
     - den: *gehören* requires the dative: *der Lehrer → dem Lehrer*.
3. Does the bread roll taste good to you (du)? → Schmeckt dir das Brötchen?
4. The new jacket fits her (Mina). → Die neue Jacke passt ihr.
- Explanation: *helfen, gehören, schmecken, passen, gefallen, danken* always take the dative.
- How to help: the app checks word by word against the solutions above and names mistakes of form (e.g. accents) with the child's words. Do not give the solution: ask about the rule (ending, gender, tense) or about a similar sentence of the page. If the app takes a version of the child's as wrong that you think is right, tell the child: the app knows only the versions above.

### Exercise dativverb-erkennen

- Task: Which sentence with *gefallen* is grammatically correct?
- Answer format: choose one answer
- Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
  - ✓ Die Geschenke gefallen der Familie Weber. – Correct! *Die Geschenke* (plural) is the subject → *gefallen*, and *die Familie* takes the dative → *der Familie*.
  - ✗ Die Familie Weber gefällt die Geschenke. – Subject and dative object are swapped here. The gifts please someone (dative).
  - ✗ Die Geschenke gefallen die Familie Weber. – *gefallen* requires the dative: feminine *die Familie* becomes *der Familie*.
- Solution: Die Geschenke gefallen der Familie Weber.
- Explanation: With *gefallen*, the verb agrees with the thing (*die Geschenke gefallen*), and the person is in the dative (*der Familie*).
